The operating system

Run this before the daily cards

The camp engine

Debate camp is not primarily a lecture sequence. It is a correction machine: you observe high-level debating, try the move, get feedback, redo the speech, and archive the lesson so the next round improves.

Observe→Model→Imitate→Record→Self-ballot→Redo→Retrieve→Transfer
KeystoneThe loop is the camp. The topic is the material you pour through it.

The daily schedule

Core block · 90 minutes

  • 10 min: review yesterday's error log
  • 15 min: concept + exemplar
  • 20 min: write/cut/flow
  • 30 min: speaking drill, recorded
  • 15 min: self-ballot + redo plan

Camp-strength block · 3 hours

  • 45 min: research or case work
  • 45 min: drill ladder
  • 45 min: mini-round or speech reconstruction
  • 30 min: redo the worst speech
  • 15 min: retrieval prompts
Ratio rule: hold yourself to about half the time speaking, drilling, receiving feedback, or redoing. Reading about PF is not the same as training PF.

01

Foundation

Days 1–3 · round architecture, flow, and ballot logic

Day 1 — Diagnostic + round architecture

Record the starting point and learn each speech's job.

Learn
  • PF is not an essay contest; it is a sequence of speeches that narrow a decision.
  • Each speech should prepare the next speech: constructive seeds the final focus, rebuttal sets summary, summary locks final focus.
Drill
BASELINE RECORDING 20 min prep · 4 min speech Pick a current or recent PF resolution. Give one constructive. Save it. ROUND LABELING one elite round Watch once. After each speech, say: build / answer / collapse / weigh. Do not evaluate quality yet. Learn the skeleton.
Underlined pointYou cannot improve honestly without a Day-1 recording.

Day 2 — Flowing: the operating system

Convert speech into a usable decision map.

Learn
  • A flow tracks the life of each argument across speeches.
  • One row = one argument chain; one column = one speech.
  • Drops, turns, and unanswered weighing should be visually obvious.
Drill
SPEED LADDER 0.75× → 1× → 1.25× or 1.5× Flow the same round three times. After each pass, close the video and reconstruct the round from the flow alone. FLOW → SUMMARY 90 sec Give a mini-summary from the flow without replaying the round.
Underlined pointIf it is not on your flow, it did not happen for practical purposes.

Day 3 — Ballot story + weighing

Build backward from the decision you want the judge to write.

Learn
  • Weighing is comparative: magnitude, probability, timeframe, scope, reversibility, prerequisite, turns case.
  • A strong ballot story is one sentence: “Vote ___ because ___ outweighs ___ by ___.”
Drill
FINAL-FOCUS FIRST Write the final focus before writing the constructive. Then ask: what contention, evidence, and crossfire concession would make this ballot true? THREE WEIGHS, SAME CLASH Weigh the same clash by probability, timeframe, and prerequisite. Notice how the round changes.
Underlined pointA contention earns space only if it helps the final ballot story.
02

Construction

Days 4–6 · research, cards, cases, and blocks

Day 4 — Topic research tree

Research the controversy, not just your side.

RESOLUTION → definitions → mechanisms → stakeholders → academic literature → government / institutional reports → think tanks and advocacy → current examples → opponent literature → hidden assumptions → predicted opponent strategies → preemptive blocks
Drill
TREE WALK For each node, write 2 bullets: what I know / what I still need. End by naming the opponent's three best arguments.
Underlined pointThe research tree ends at their best case, not yours.

Day 5 — Honest cards + case construction

Convert research into two defensible constructives.

Card standard
  • Accurate tag, not power-tagged.
  • Author, date, source, qualifications, and access information recorded.
  • Quotation preserves context and does not reverse the author.
  • You can explain the warrant without reading the card.
CASE TEST For each contention, write: 1. uniqueness 2. link 3. internal link 4. impact 5. weighing hook 6. expected answer 7. frontline
Underlined pointEvidence does not replace explanation; it licenses explanation.

Day 6 — Block-file engineering

Turn answers into a searchable, season-long asset.

[OPPONENT CLAIM] 1. No uniqueness 2. No link 3. Link turn / impact turn 4. Impact defense 5. Evidence 6. Weighing comparison 7. Crossfire question 8. Summary extension 9. Final Focus extension 10. Lay-judge plain-English version
Drill
BIG FIVE BLOCKS Build full blocks against the five most likely opponent arguments. PULL TEST Can you find the right block and give the 20-second version without reading it?
Underlined pointA block file is not a pile of answers. It is a database for round speed.
03

Execution

Days 7–11 · speeches and in-round control

Day 7 — Rebuttal + reconstruction

Learn elite execution by imitating, comparing, and redoing.

Drill
CHAMPION RECONSTRUCTION 1. Watch a strong rebuttal once. 2. Pause and deliver your version from the flow. 3. Compare: what did they prioritize that you missed? 4. Redo until the structure matches, not the words. SECOND REBUTTAL RULE Frontline your case before extending offense against theirs.
Underlined pointSpeech reconstruction is the debate equivalent of transcribing a jazz solo.

Day 8 — Summary: winning by subtraction

Collapse to the arguments you can actually win.

COLLAPSE LADDER 4 possible voters → choose 2 → choose 1. For each cut, say what you are dropping and why. SUMMARY TEMPLATE 1. judge, the round is about ___ 2. we win ___ because ___ 3. their answer fails because ___ 4. this outweighs on ___ 5. even if they win ___, it does not access the ballot because ___
Underlined pointThe summary is defined by disciplined dropping, not heroic coverage.

Day 9 — Final Focus: the ballot aloud

Make the judge's decision feel inevitable.

ONE FLOW, THREE FINAL FOCUSES Deliver three 2-minute final focuses from the same flow: A. probability ballot B. timeframe ballot C. prerequisite ballot MIRROR TEST Every final focus claim must have appeared in summary. If not, cut it.
Underlined pointFinal Focus is not another rebuttal; it is the judge's RFD spoken aloud.

Day 10 — Crossfire: setup, not theater

Ask questions that feed later speeches.

QUESTION → USE TEST For each crossfire question, fill in: Question: Expected answer: Speech line it enables: If no speech line follows, delete the question. FIVE QUESTION TYPES clarify mechanism · expose missing link · force concession · test evidence · set weighing
Underlined pointA crossfire win only matters if a later speech uses it.

04

Mastery

Days 12–14 · rounds, diagnosis, retrieval, transfer

Day 12 — Rounds + root-cause analysis

Stop fixing symptoms; fix the system that caused them.

CAUSE CHAIN Symptom: dropped turn Why? flow was messy Why? missed the link-turn signal Why? didn't understand the mechanism Root cause: research gap + turn-ID weakness Drill: D4 mechanism map + D7 turn reconstruction
Round work
  • One full round on each side, ideally with a partner, parent, teammate, or AI opponent.
  • Write the ballot before discussing who “should” have won.
  • Redo only the worst speech from each round.
Underlined pointThe question is not “what did I drop?” It is “what caused the drop?”

Day 13 — Retrieval + clarity

Make the file live in your head and come out clearly.

CLOSED-FILE PROMPTS 60 sec each Five answers to their best argument. Three turns on the central mechanism. Two weighing comparisons on the key clash. One lay explanation of our ballot story. CLARITY TEST Record a fast summary. Flow your own recording. If you can't flow it, the judge can't either.
Underlined pointIf you must open the file, the response is not tournament-ready.

Day 14 — Tournament simulation + transfer

Stress-test the system and prove it travels to a new topic.

TOURNAMENT SIM Round 1: Pro Round 2: Con Round 3: redo your weaker side Optional Round 4: lay-judge adaptation only TRANSFER TEST New resolution. 60 minutes. Build a mini research tree, ballot story, and one contention per side. RE-BASELINE Re-record the Day-1 constructive and compare: flowability, warranting, weighing, confidence.
Underlined pointThe camp ends; the weekly loop continues.

Parent / partner mode

How a non-debater can still help

What to listen for

  • Can you explain their main reason after the speech?
  • Did they compare their impact to the other side?
  • Did they sound like they were answering or just reading?
  • Did the final focus tell you how to vote?

What not to do

  • Do not judge whether the topic claim is personally persuasive.
  • Do not reward speed you cannot follow.
  • Do not fix the speech for them; ask what they meant.
  • Do not skip redos.
Parent ballot prompt: “I would vote ___ because ___. The part I could not follow was ___. The clearest comparison was ___. The next redo should make ___ simpler.”
